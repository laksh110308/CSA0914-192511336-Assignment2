
QUESTION 6 – PRIORITY, ORDER AND FAIRNESS IN A QUEUE

1. OBJECTIVE

To develop a hospital emergency triage system using Java PriorityQueue. The system prioritizes patients based on severity and arrival order.

2. PRIORITY RULES

1. Severity 1 receives the highest priority.
2. Severity 2 receives the next priority.
3. Severity 3 receives medium priority.
4. Severity 4 receives low priority.
5. Severity 5 receives the lowest priority.
6. Patients with equal severity are treated in arrival order.

3. DATA STRUCTURE USED

PriorityQueue is used to maintain patients according to their severity and arrival time.

Comparator is used to compare patients.

A counter variable maintains the arrival order.

4. EXPERIMENTAL DATASET

Eight patients were used for testing:

Arun - Severity 3
Bala - Severity 1

5. Arun - Severity 3

7. TIME COMPLEXITY ANALYSIS

PriorityQueue:
- Adding a patient: O(log n)
- Removing the next patient: O(log n)
- Viewing the next patient: O(1)
- Searching for a patient: O(n)
- Space complexity: O(n)

Sorted LinkedList:
- Adding a patient: O(n)
- Removing the next patient: O(1)
- Space complexity: O(n)

Five FIFO Queues:
- Adding a patient: O(1)
- Removing the next patient: O(1)
- Space complexity: O(n)

8. STARVATION AND FAIRNESS

Starvation occurs when low-priority patients wait indefinitely because higher-priority patients continuously arrive.

Aging is proposed as a solution. The priority of a patient can be improved after every 30 minutes of waiting.

The priority queue must be rebuilt whenever effective priorities are updated.

9. DIRECT PRIORITYQUEUE DISPLAY

Printing a PriorityQueue directly does not guarantee treatment order because its internal structure is a heap, not a completely sorted list.

Repeatedly using poll() on a copied queue displays the correct treatment order.

10. DEPLOYMENT DECISION

The PriorityQueue design can support a hospital receiving 5,000 patients per day because insertion and removal operations take O(log n) time.

For real-world deployment, additional features such as patient record storage, multiple doctor support, concurrency control and emergency overrides are required.

11. CONCLUSION

The experiment demonstrates the use of Java PriorityQueue for hospital emergency triage. Patients are treated according to severity, and patients with equal severity are treated in their arrival order.

The growth investigation evaluates insertion and removal performance at different queue sizes. Aging is proposed to reduce starvation and improve fairness.

Therefore, PriorityQueue provides an efficient approach for managing emergency patient priorities.
